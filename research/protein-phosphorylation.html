Finding protein phosphorylation sites when evidence is incomplete.

A protein’s amino-acid sequence carries clues about where phosphorylation may occur. My undergraduate thesis explored how those clues could complement mass-spectrometry analysis when a spectrum cannot localize a site confidently on its own.

Question → method → evidence

The biological problem

Phosphorylation often occurs at serine, threonine, or tyrosine residues. Mass spectrometry can identify phosphorylated peptides, but some spectra lack the ions needed to pin the modification to one specific residue. In that case, sequence-based prediction can provide additional evidence—not a definitive experimental answer.

What I built

I prepared candidate sites as 111-residue windows centered on an S, T, or Y position, removed duplicate and contradictory labels, and addressed class imbalance through repeated downsampling. An ESM‑2 protein-language-model encoder generated sequence representations; a fully connected neural network (FCNN) mapped those representations to a phosphorylation probability.

For the localization study, I combined these predictions with PTMiner outputs. When a spectrum lacked site-determining ions, the model scored candidate S/T/Y residues on its matched peptide and selected the highest-probability candidate as a proposed localization.

What the thesis found

On the thesis’s held-out site-prediction comparison, ESM‑2 + FCNN achieved an F1 score of 0.862, compared with 0.830 for DeepPhos under the reported setup. In a separate mass-spectrometry analysis, fine-tuning improved agreement with high-confidence PTMiner localizations, although the thesis did not establish new experimentally verified sites.

What this taught me

The project brought together biological data curation, pretrained representations, model comparison, and a realistic downstream use case. It also showed the limit of sequence-only prediction: probabilities can help prioritize an ambiguous site, but they cannot create missing physical evidence from a spectrum.

Source: Minghua Liu, undergraduate thesis, “Prediction and Localization of Protein Phosphorylation Sites Based on Deep Learning” (Beijing Information Science & Technology University, 2024). The full thesis is not posted here because its original PDF includes personal records.
