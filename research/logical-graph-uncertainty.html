When different answers are not really in conflict.

A model may answer the same question in several ways. Counting those answers as separate disagreements can make it look less reliable than it is. Logical Graph Uncertainty asks a more precise question: which answers can logically coexist?

Read the arXiv preprint ↗Back to selected research ↗
Question → method → evidence

The question

Suppose a language model answers “in the 1920s” in one sample and “in 1926” in another. These are not paraphrases, but they are not contradictions either: one answer is simply more specific. Semantic entropy can treat them as different outcomes and inflate uncertainty.

I worked with co-authors on a way to distinguish diverse wording and specificity from genuine conflict. The goal is a better signal for deciding when a generated answer deserves verification.

What we built

We first group equivalent answers. Then we add directed links when one answer entails another and conflict links when two answers cannot both hold. Probability mass is aggregated along implication paths toward the strongest supported hypotheses. Finally, incompatibility among those hypotheses raises the uncertainty score.

What the evidence suggests

The preprint evaluates hallucination discrimination and selective prediction across TriviaQA, SQuAD, BioASQ, and Natural Questions. LGU has the best average rank among the compared uncertainty measures. Its clearest gains appear when sampled answers have meaningful logical structure.

+7.1 ppLargest reported AUROC gain over semantic entropy on logically structured questions.
+3.5 ppLargest reported AUARC gain over semantic entropy on the same type of subset.

These are the paper’s reported maximum gains on a subset, not average gains across every dataset or model.

Why I care about it

For reliable AI, an uncertainty score is useful only if it separates answers that really need checking from variation that is harmless. This project made that distinction a first-class part of the method. A next step is to study whether such scores can guide when an agent should verify, retrieve more evidence, or defer a decision.
